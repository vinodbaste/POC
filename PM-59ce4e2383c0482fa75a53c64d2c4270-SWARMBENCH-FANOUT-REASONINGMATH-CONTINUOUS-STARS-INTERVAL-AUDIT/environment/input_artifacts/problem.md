You observed the night sky on (N) consecutive nights.

On night (i), exactly (A_i) stars were visible.

Due to peculiarities of the Solar System, each star is visible during a single continuous interval of nights. That is, every star has integers (L,R) satisfying
[
1 \le L \le R \le N
]
such that the star is visible exactly on nights
[
L,L+1,\dots,R.
]

Different collections of stars may produce the same nightly visibility counts.

Let:

* (m) be the minimum possible number of distinct stars observed across all (N) nights,
* (M) be the maximum possible number of distinct stars observed across all (N) nights.

Given
[
A=[4,1,3,5,2,2,3,1,5,6,2,1],
]
determine
[
m+M
]
and justify your reasoning carefully.
