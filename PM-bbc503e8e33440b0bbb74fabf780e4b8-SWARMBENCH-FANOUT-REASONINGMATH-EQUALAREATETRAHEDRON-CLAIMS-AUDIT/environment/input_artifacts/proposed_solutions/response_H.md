The standard equifacial theorem applied to hypothesis (2) gives opposite edges equal in three pairs $AB=CD$, $AC=BD$, $AD=BC$ — (a) holds. The four faces are SSS-congruent on these three pair-lengths — (b) holds. From $V = \tfrac{1}{3} S_X h_X$ with all four $S_X$ equal, the four altitudes all equal $3V/S$ — (c) holds.

For (d): an equifacial tetrahedron admits a rectangular-box embedding with $A,B,C,D$ at alternating corners $(\pm p, \pm q, \pm r)$. The centroid is then the origin, and every vertex sits at distance $\sqrt{p^2 + q^2 + r^2}$ from the origin, so the origin is also the circumcenter. Centroid = circumcenter — (d) holds.

The area-weighted incenter formula $I = \frac{\sum S_X V_X}{\sum S_X}$ collapses to the centroid when all $S_X$ are equal — (h) holds.

(e), (f), (g) fail in the general case: a non-regular box-embedded equifacial tetrahedron with $p \ne q \ne r$ gives scalene faces and three distinct edge-pair lengths.
