By the classical equifacial tetrahedron theorem, equal face areas force opposite edges equal in pairs $AB=CD$, $AC=BD$, $AD=BC$, giving (a). Each face then has its edges drawn from the same three pair-lengths $\{p,q,r\}$, so by SSS the four faces are pairwise congruent, giving (b). From $V = \tfrac{1}{3} S_X h_X$ with all $S_X$ equal, all four altitudes coincide at $h = 3V/S$, giving (c).

For (d), an equifacial tetrahedron embeds in a rectangular box $[-p,p]\times[-q,q]\times[-r,r]$ at alternating corners; the centroid sits at the origin and each vertex is at distance $\sqrt{p^2+q^2+r^2}$ from the origin, so origin = circumcenter as well — centroid = circumcenter. (d) holds.

(e) fails: the inscribed-sphere touch points are NOT the face centroids in general — for the box parametrisation with $p \ne q \ne r$ the foot of perpendicular from $G$ drifts off the face centroid. (f) and (g) fail (the box construction gives scalene faces and three distinct pair-lengths for $p \ne q \ne r$, so not equilateral and not regular).

For (h), the area-weighted incenter formula $I = (\sum S_X V_X)/(\sum S_X)$ collapses to the centroid when all $S_X$ are equal, so (h) holds.
