From equal face areas the classical equifacial-tetrahedron theorem gives opposite edges equal — (a) holds. The faces then share three pair-lengths, so SSS gives (b). The volume identity $V = \tfrac{1}{3} S_X h_X$ with all $S_X$ equal gives (c).

The area-weighted incenter formula collapses to the centroid when all $S_X$ coincide, giving (h).

I leave (d) out of my final answer: I do not see a clean proof that the centroid equals the circumcenter without an extra symmetry hypothesis I cannot establish here. Equidistance to the four vertices needs more than equal face areas alone, and I prefer to be conservative.

(e), (f), (g) all fail in the general equifacial case.
